The playbook

Blame a word
nobody defines.

Nobody says exactly what “woke” means. That's what makes it useful.

Diversity training goes under it. So do climate teams, working from home and whole universities. Anything you dislike can join the list.

Because the word is vague, you can attach an enormous number to it, like £7 billion a year, and the number is hard to check.

And because it sounds like an idea, it's easy to forget that the people told to find alternative careers are council staff.

So let's check the number.

The reality

Follow
the money.

£27.1m

was the total spent on equality, diversity and inclusion across 95 civil service organisations in 2022-23, according to a Cabinet Office review commissioned by the last Conservative government.

Reform says its £7 billion figure covers the whole public sector, based on an estimate by the Conservative Way Forward group. The Cabinet Office review covers the civil service only.

380

people worked in equality, diversity and inclusion roles across a civil service of more than half a million, the same review found.

£70,842

was the civil service's total reported spending on external equality, diversity and inclusion activity in 2024/25, before VAT. It included antisemitism training, memberships of a network for staff who are carers, a Holocaust memorial event and a Christian fellowship event.

4 of 10

councils Reform took over in 2025 had no equality officers before it arrived, according to Freedom of Information answers obtained by openDemocracy. The councils that did have a few still employed them a year later.

Three councils did not respond.

s.149

of the Equality Act 2010 requires public bodies to consider how their decisions affect discrimination and equal opportunity. Some equality work isn't a political choice. It's a legal duty.

Is any of it wasted? Possibly. The Conservative government that ordered the review said £27.1 million was not value for money, and it tightened the rules. That's a fair debate about millions of pounds.

It isn't a debate about billions. When Reform took control of Kent County Council, one of its own cabinet members told the Financial Times that people had expected “these huge costs we could cut away, but there just aren't.”

Reform promised billions hidden in “woke”.

The official figures found millions, and one of Reform's own councillors said there wasn't much more to find.
