The playbook

Swap evidence
for doubt.

You don't have to disprove the evidence. You just have to make it sound uncertain.

“How do we know?” turns decades of measurement into a matter of opinion.

“The power of the sun” swaps something scientists have measured for something that feels obvious.

And when an independent watchdog might check your numbers, you float the idea that the country would be better off without it.

Each move says the same thing: the people who study this for a living are no better a guide than your gut.

Here's what they actually found.

The reality

How do
we know?

2021

The UN's Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change, drawing on thousands of studies, concluded that it is unequivocal that human activity has warmed the atmosphere, the oceans and the land.

1950s

Since then, the energy Earth receives from the Sun has shown no net increase. Global temperatures have risen sharply over the same period, so it's extremely unlikely the Sun caused recent warming.

NASA. The Royal Society reaches the same conclusion from 40 years of satellite measurements.

2026

brought the UK's hottest summer on record, with five separate heatwaves, according to the Met Office.

£65bn

was the emergency bond-buying scheme the Bank of England announced in 2022, after a mini-Budget of unfunded tax cuts sent markets into turmoil. The Chancellor had asked the OBR not to publish its usual independent forecast alongside it.

Economists say markets read the missing forecast as a sign the government was ignoring its own watchdogs. A Bank of England policymaker said at the time that sidelining the OBR made everyone's information worse and added to the uncertainty.

Experts get things wrong. Forecasts miss, and scientific estimates get revised. Questioning experts is healthy, and so is asking them to show their working.

To be fair to Reform, Robert Jenrick has since said the party supports the OBR's purpose and would keep it, while changing how it works.

But there's a difference between challenging the evidence and waving it away. “How do we know?” has an answer. People measured it, for decades, all over the world.

Reform asks how we know.

The experts wrote it down.
