The playbook

Make them
a threat.

The argument begins with something almost impossible to disagree with: children should be safe.

So should women.

There are legitimate debates about medicine for young people, single-sex services and how sex and gender should be treated in law.

But watch what happens to those debates.

A disputed medical treatment becomes “child abuse”.

A disagreement about policy becomes “gender ideology”.

A dispute over access to particular spaces becomes a question of keeping women safe.

And a small minority of the population becomes associated, again and again, with danger to somebody else.

The political offer then becomes simple: you need protecting — and Reform will protect you.

The reality

Debate the policy.
Not the bogeyman.

≈ 1 in 200

people aged 16 and over in England and Wales are estimated to have a gender identity different from their sex registered at birth. The ONS cautions that the Census figure is not a precise population estimate, but says around 1 in 200 is broadly consistent with other sources.

3,809

transgender hate crimes were recorded by police in England and Wales in the year ending March 2025, excluding Metropolitan Police data from the headline series.

Not routine

Puberty blockers are not routinely available through the NHS for under-18s with gender incongruence or gender dysphoria. Routine commissioning ended in 2024 after the Cass Review found the evidence base inadequate.

A clinical trial

PATHWAYS is research intended to establish better evidence about puberty suppression. After safety concerns were raised in 2026, the medicines regulator required changes to the protocol and strengthened safeguards before approving it to regulatory standards.

None of this means every policy advocated in the name of trans inclusion is correct.

The evidence around puberty blockers was weak. The NHS changed its policy. The government restricted prescribing. Regulators scrutinised the proposed clinical trial and required stronger safeguards.

Those are facts.

But they do not turn a clinical trial into “state-sanctioned child abuse”. Nor do disagreements about toilets, changing rooms or medical policy establish that trans people collectively present a danger to women or children.

You can debate policy without turning people into a threat.
