The playbook

Turn people
into a threat.

Start with a complicated system involving asylum claims, irregular migration, legal obligations, accommodation, appeals and individual circumstances.

Then remove the complexity.

They become “illegal migrants”.

Their arrival becomes an “invasion”.

Accommodation becomes “luxury hotels”.

Housing people becomes “dumping” them in your community.

Different people, circumstances and legal cases become one threatening group: them.

The reality

An invasion?
Look at the numbers.

40%

of people claiming asylum in the year ending June 2026 had arrived in the UK by small boat.

41%

had previously entered the UK with a visa or other form of permission before claiming asylum.

60%

of small-boat asylum claims receiving a substantive initial decision since 2018 had resulted in asylum, another form of protection, or other leave by the end of 2025.

17%

of people receiving asylum support were being accommodated in hotels at the end of June 2026.

13 in 10,000

UK residents were asylum seekers receiving asylum support at the end of June 2026.

Irregular migration is a real policy issue. So is the cost and operation of Britain's asylum system.

But those questions are not answered by pretending that every asylum seeker arrived in a small boat, lives in a hotel, has no legitimate protection claim or represents a threat to the community in which they are housed.

Reform gives you a much simpler picture:

A boat. A hotel. A dangerous man. An invasion.

The government's own statistics describe something considerably more complicated.
