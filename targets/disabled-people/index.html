The playbook

Make it
personal.

Notice how the argument is constructed.

It doesn't begin with welfare statistics or the circumstances of people who cannot work.

It asks you to imagine your neighbour.

You are leaving for work. Their curtains are closed. You are paying taxes. They are receiving benefits.

One person is working. One person isn't. One is paying. One is taking.

Reform then supplies the conclusion: “This is unfair.”

The reality

Look beyond
the curtains.

5.5 million

disabled people are in work in the UK.

29.7 points

is the employment-rate gap between disabled and non-disabled people.

7.6%

of workless disabled people move into work in a year, compared with 28.3% of workless non-disabled people.

1.2%

of Personal Independence Payment expenditure was estimated by the Department for Work and Pensions as functional-needs fraud in 2025/26.

A closed curtain tells you nothing about the person behind it.

They may already work. They may be unable to work. They may be trying to get back into work in a labour market where disabled people are substantially less likely to be employed.

Reform asks you to look at your neighbour's curtains.

We'd rather you looked at the evidence.
